Inside terminal AWhat moves

One fibre tip on the stage. Nothing else.

  1. 01Fibre-network transceiver (SFP). A mass-produced plug-in module with its own laser and light detector, made for fibre-optic networks. It stays on the fixed frame.
  2. 02Media converter. Turns the SFP into an ordinary Ethernet port, so a camera, a Raspberry Pi or a laptop plugs straight in.
  3. 03Optical fibre. A short patch cord from the SFP. Its free end is the only thing on the stage — it weighs a few grams.
  4. 04XY Lab stage. Holds the fibre tip and moves it sideways and up-down in steps of tens of nanometres. No sliding parts: the printed flexures bend.
  5. 05Lens. Fixed one focal length in front of the tip. It turns the light from the tip into a straight beam, and focuses the incoming beam back to a point.
Two colours, one pathFull duplex

Both ends talk and listen at the same time.

The link uses a matched pair of BiDi transceivers. Each sends on one wavelength and listens on the other, through a single fibre. A tiny filter inside the module keeps the outgoing and incoming colours apart.

So the two directions share one beam, travelling opposite ways at once — like a two-lane road, not a walkie-talkie. The video only goes from A to B, but the network needs the return lane to confirm the link is alive.

And because light retraces its path, the stage only has to do one thing: put A’s fibre tip exactly where B’s light lands. A’s own beam then points straight back at B.

Honest limitsWhat it won’t do

Where the idea stops.

  • It is a concept. The stage exists and is measured; the kilometre link is the goal we are building towards, starting at tens of metres.
  • Slow disturbances only. The stage corrects drift, sway and bumps up to about 20 Hz. The fast shimmer of hot air over a kilometre is faster than that and is not corrected.
  • Weather wins. Fog and heavy rain block every laser link. A radio link as backup covers those hours.
  • The fixed end must stay still. For long rooftop links, the second terminal gets its own stage too.
  • Eye safety first. Low power, beams above head height and away from flight paths.
