Good to know

  • Figures come from video-tracking characterisation and may vary between builds. Interferometric validation is the next step.
  • In lithography, feature size is set by your optics. The stage decides where each field lands.
  • Power comes from a 12 V, 2 A DC adapter, which is not in the box.
  • Travel is about 3 mm a side. If you need centimetres, you want a bearing stage.

Before you orderAsk us more

The things buyers ask.

Does it come with the electronics?

Yes. Both motors come fitted, with the stepper drivers and your choice of microcontroller in the box. The one thing you add is power: any 12 V, 2 A DC adapter with a barrel plug. ₹2,500 covers everything else, including shipping within India.

Arduino Nano or ESP32?

Both drive the stage. The Nano is the simpler board and the one in our parts list. The ESP32 is faster and adds Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, which helps if you want to fold the stage into a larger, networked setup. Same price either way.

What is the difference between XY Lab and XY One?

XY One was the first stage, the one that showed a printed flexure resolving increments below 20 nm. XY Lab is the body that came next: a cross-shaped base that bolts flat to an optical table, a closed frame around the slide holder, and guide rods that stop the drive blocks yawing. XY Lab is the one we build for sale.

Why buy it if the files are free?

Because a 0.5 mm flexure blade is unforgiving to print, and your time is worth more than the difference. If you already have a well-tuned printer, build your own — that is exactly what the files are for.

How were the numbers measured?

A marker on the slide holder, filmed at 20 frames per second and tracked frame by frame against a calibrated scale with sub-pixel centroid fitting. The full runs and their caveats are on the XY Lab page.

Can I order several, or something custom?

Yes. For more than one stage, a modified body, a different mounting pattern or a lab set, write to us or call. A human reads every message.
